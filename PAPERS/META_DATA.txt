TITLE: **Applying TRANSFORMATICS in GENETICS: The Mathematics of Genome Sequencing**

**ABSTRACT:**

This work is part of an upcoming book(via Taylor and Francis), "Applying TRANSFORMATICS in GENETICS" by the author. Sequencing is such a central concept in genetics that it surely warrants its own chapter in a book on any modern mathematics of genetics such as is being developed and being formalized in this book. Moreover, it is not just in genetics that the idea of sequencing finds immediate relevance, just as with many of the other concepts being treated of in this book, much of what mechanics and theory is being developed about sequence processing, analysis and in general, the algebra of sequences, shall find many useful and sometimes fundamental applications in domains such as computer science, physics, pure and applied mathematics, medicine, economics, etc. In this chapter though, focus is on the problem of genome sequencing, with special attention being given to identifying the core problems underlying such sequencing as it is encountered in contemporary genetics literature and practice, and then, leveraging the new mathematics of transformatics, to develop entirely new formalisms and theory concerning how such sequencing might be accomplished, but also how it might be applied. We cover the matter of sequence alignment, with special, deep treatment of the issue of computing the maximum common subsequence from any collection of disparate sequences. We then handle the issue of computing a consensus sequence given any two sequences well aligned and with potential disparities in the overlapping sections (specifically for genetic sequences here), then finally apply these two solutions to the issue of formally specifying the problem of genome sequencing, to which we provide a solution in form of a special sequence transformer that we call the genome sequencer. Finally, we see how the theory thus developed might be used to implement such critical applications such as DNA or genetic tests, and how these ideas culminate in a law about the unique identity of any entity for which there exists some kind of genome sequence.

**KEYWORDS:** Modern Mathematics, Transformatics, Sequence Algebra, Sequence Analysis, Sequence Processors, DNA, Genome Sequencing, Sequence Transformers.
